Why a pantry list helps

Throwing food away is usually a memory problem, not a cooking problem. You forget the spinach exists, the leftovers get pushed to the back, and a week later it’s the bin. Writing it down once means you remember it.

How to set up your pantry in ten minutes

You don’t need to log your whole kitchen. A short list you keep up to date beats a perfect list you abandon.

In Matlyst

Paste a link or share a post, then check the draft before you save.

Read more
  1. Start with what spoils first. Fresh produce, dairy, opened jars, and anything you bought for one recipe and have left over.
  2. Add an expiry date where the package has one. For fresh food without a date, add the day you bought it and use your judgment.
  3. Add the freezer next. Log what is in it with a date, so the oldest goes first.
  4. Skip the staples you never run out of, like salt and oil, until you want them on the list.
  5. Update it when you shop and when you cook. Two minutes then saves you ten later.

What Matlyst doesn’t do

  • It doesn’t scan barcodes. You add items by hand.
  • It doesn’t know what is really in your fridge. The list is only as good as the last time you updated it.
  • It doesn’t decide food safety for you. An expiry date is a guide. Use the storage rules below and your own judgment.

How long food keeps

These are the basic numbers from FoodSafety.gov, the U.S. government’s food safety site. It also lists storage times for hundreds of specific foods.

WhatRule
Refrigerator temperature40°F (4°C) or below
Freezer temperature0°F (−18°C)
Leftover cooked meat or poultry, and pizza, in the fridge3 to 4 days

Source: FoodSafety.gov, cold food storage chart, last reviewed September 19, 2023. Freezer times are for quality only. Food kept continuously at 0°F (−18°C) stays safe.

One more habit from professional kitchens: raw meat goes on the lowest shelf, below food that won’t be cooked again, so nothing can drip onto it. As a refrigeration technician I saw it done on purpose, every day. It costs nothing, and it works.

A ten-minute Friday check

A pantry list stays true only if you look at it on a schedule. Pick one day, and make it the day before you plan the week or shop. Friday works well for many households because the weekend is when you cook more and shop more.

  1. Open the pantry and read the days left. Start with whatever expires first. That is your cooking order for the next few days.
  2. Open the fridge and match it to the list. Anything in the fridge that is not on the list gets added. Anything on the list that is gone gets marked Used up.
  3. Look in the freezer. Is there something you forgot about? Put it on this week’s plan.
  4. Pick two dinners from what you found. Look at What can you make? in the pantry, or search your saved recipes by an ingredient, then put those recipes on the meal plan.
  5. Only then write the grocery list. Send the planned recipes to the list and shop for the gaps.

It takes about ten minutes once the list exists. The order matters: look first, plan second, shop last. Most wasted food comes from doing it the other way around.

First in, first out, at home

“First in, first out,” or FIFO, is how professional kitchens rotate stock. The oldest delivery gets used first, and the new one goes behind it. Patrick, who founded Matlyst, worked as a refrigeration technician and saw that rule applied in commercial kitchens. At home it needs no equipment, only a habit.

What it looks like in your kitchen

  • When you unpack groceries, move the older package to the front and put the new one behind it.
  • Keep one shelf or one box for “eat this first.” Open jars, half a lemon, and the last two slices of ham go there.
  • In the freezer, write the date on every bag, and keep the oldest on top.
  • When you add an item to the pantry list, set Days until it expires from the date on the package. Matlyst does not guess this number for you, so the habit is yours.

The list and the shelf do the same job. The shelf shows you what to grab, and the list tells you how many days are left. If you only do one of them, do the one you will actually keep up.

Where things live: fridge, cupboard, and freezer

Where you keep an item matters. Here is the short version. For storage times on a specific food, use the FoodSafety.gov storage chart.

The fridge

Keep it at 40°F (4°C) or colder. In the commercial kitchens Patrick worked around, raw meat, poultry, and fish went on the lowest shelf so nothing could drip onto other food, and that rule works just as well at home. Cooked leftovers and ready-to-eat food go above them.

The cupboard

Dry goods like rice, pasta, flour, canned tomatoes, and spices belong here. In Matlyst, choose the category Dry goods for them, and they are saved without an expiration date, so they never crowd the top of the list.

The freezer

Keep it at 0°F (-18°C). Freeze food while it is still fresh, not on its last day, and check the FoodSafety.gov chart for how long a specific food keeps there.

Why this belongs on your pantry list

A list that says only “chicken” is not much help. A list that says “chicken thighs (freezer)” with a countdown beside it tells you where to look and how long you planned to keep it. The item name is free text, so add the place to the name if it helps. The category (Meat, Dairy, Vegetables, or Dry goods) decides the suggested number of days, and Dry goods get none.

How to organize the freezer so you actually use it

A freezer fails when you cannot see what is in it. Food at the bottom stays for months, and the same three things get used from the top. A few small changes fix that.

  1. Keep the front for what goes first. A freezer packed to the lid hides its bottom layer. Put the oldest items where you see them when you open the door.
  2. Label with the name and the date. Use a marker on the bag or a strip of tape. “Chili, Mar 3” takes five seconds. An unlabeled bag is a guess later.
  3. Zones, not piles. One area for meat, one for vegetables, one for cooked meals, one for bread. Use a box or a bin for each.
  4. Freeze in portions. Portion sizes you will actually use, like two servings, so you thaw what you need and not a whole block.
  5. Put it on the list. Add each freezer item to your pantry with the number of days you plan to keep it. When the countdown gets short, you know to eat it soon.

Remember that the free plan shows 5 items. If your freezer is large, start with the 5 oldest or the 5 you most want to use up, and see whether the habit sticks.

How days left work in Matlyst

The pantry does not read dates from packages. It counts down from a number you give it, so it helps to know exactly what it does with that number.

What you seeWhat it meansWhat to do
A number of daysDays left until the expiration you setPlan the lowest numbers first.
Use tomorrow!One day leftPut it on tomorrow’s dinner.
Use today!The countdown has reached todayCook it tonight, or freeze it if that suits the food.
ExpiredThe date you set has passedJudge the food itself, with the storage rules, your eyes, and your nose.
Dry goodsSaved without an expiration dateNothing to watch. It stays on the list until you mark it used up.

When you add an item by hand, you type the number in Days until it expires. Matlyst fills in the amount and unit for many common items, and names like rice or pasta are saved as Dry goods without a date. Items you check off the grocery list move into the pantry with a rough default based on whether they are meat, dairy, or vegetables. That default is not a reading of the package, so if a number is far off, remove the item and add it again with the right one.

If you turn on Expiration reminders in Settings, Matlyst sends a notification at 8 AM when something in your pantry expires today or tomorrow. The countdown is only as good as the number you entered, and it is a reminder, not a safety check. Cooked meat and poultry leftovers keep 3 to 4 days in the fridge, according to FoodSafety.gov.

You will not log everything, and you should not try. Rank by how much you lose when it goes bad.

Log itWhy
Fresh produce and herbsEasy to forget in the drawer, and you often buy more than one recipe needs.
Meat, poultry, and fishCosts the most to waste, so it is worth planning early in the week.
Dairy and opened jarsThey are often pushed to the back after opening.
LeftoversEasy to lose track of, and cooked meat and poultry keep 3 to 4 days in the fridge.
Freezer itemsYou will not remember them in a month without a date.
Dry staplesLast of all. They are saved without an expiration date, so log them only when you want them on the list.

When the list is out of date

Every pantry list drifts. Do not treat it as failure, because it is the normal state of a list kept by hand. Here is what to do.

You find items that are not on the list

Add them now. Do not wait for a perfect moment. It takes a few seconds, and it is the cheapest time to do it.

The list says you have something you do not

Mark it Used up, and notice why. Did you use it and forget to update? If so, make updating part of cooking: after you cook, tap Update pantry in cook mode and pick what you used.

You have not opened it in weeks

Do not try to catch up. Tap Clear pantry, and start again with only the fresh food you want to use first. A short, true list is better than a long, old one. In a shared pantry, clearing also removes the items others added, so agree on it first.

A date is wrong

Remove the item and add it again with the right number of days. The countdown starts from what you entered, so a wrong number gives a wrong countdown.

You are sharing the list and it is a mess

Agree on one rule: whoever uses the last of something removes it. In a Pro household of up to 6 people, changes show up live for everyone, so a removal by one person is seen by the others.

What a pantry list does not solve

It helps to be honest about the limits, so you do not blame the list for things it cannot do.

  • It does not cook for you. A list of what you have does not tell you what to make, unless you search or ask. Planning is still your job.
  • It does not change what you buy. If you buy ten things for one recipe every week, the list will show it, but it will not stop you.
  • It cannot see inside your fridge. There is no barcode scanning. Items you check off the grocery list move in on their own, and Update pantry in cook mode removes what you used, but everything else changes only when you change it. It is only as true as your last update.
  • It is not a food safety tool. It counts down from a number you entered. Whether food is still good to eat is a judgment you make with the storage rules, and with your eyes and nose.
  • It does not replace a clean fridge. A fridge that is packed, warm, or never cleaned will cause waste that no list fixes.

What it does do is make forgotten food visible. For many kitchens, that is most of the problem. If you also want a place for the recipes themselves, see the recipe organizer app, and for a guide to choosing one, read how to choose a recipe organizer app.

Common mistakes

  • Logging only fresh food. The freezer and the dry cupboard waste money too.
  • Never updating the list. Remove items when they are used, or the list stops being true.
  • Buying duplicates. Check the pantry before you shop.

Pantry questions

Does Matlyst scan barcodes?

No. You add pantry items by hand.

Is the pantry free?

On the free plan, the pantry shows 5 items. Matlyst Pro adds more, along with Ask Matlyst and household sharing.

Can Matlyst suggest dinner from what I have?

Yes, with Matlyst Pro. In Ask Matlyst, Cook with what I have builds dinner ideas from your pantry. It uses AI and has a monthly allowance, so check the result before you cook.

Does the pantry stop me from buying duplicates?

When you add a recipe to your grocery list, items in your pantry are unselected and marked “In your pantry,” so you can see what you already have before you shop.

Does it work offline?

You can read your recipes and grocery list offline for up to 30 days after the last sync. Editing needs a connection.

How long do leftovers keep in the fridge?

According to FoodSafety.gov, leftover cooked meat or poultry keeps 3 to 4 days in a refrigerator at 40°F (4°C) or below.
