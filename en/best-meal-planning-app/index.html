Best meal planning apps: seven meal planners compared

We make Matlyst, so we have a stake in this. That’s why every fact here has a source and a date. Here are seven meal planning apps you can get in the US, compared on what the apps say about themselves.

If you want to plan with your own recipes and get one grocery list for the week, Matlyst, Paprika, Plan to Eat, and Recipe Keeper do that, and AnyList does with AnyList Complete. If you want the app to make the plan, Samsung Food (with Samsung Food+) and Recipe Keeper say they can. If you plan around leftovers, Plan to Eat says it handles them. If you want it free, the meal plan is free in Matlyst, ReciMe, and Samsung Food.

The short answer: pick by what you need

  • You plan with your own recipes: Matlyst, Paprika, Plan to Eat, and Recipe Keeper put your saved recipes on the days and send the week to the grocery list. AnyList does the same with AnyList Complete.
  • You want the app to make the plan: Samsung Food+ offers weekly plans made with AI. Recipe Keeper can make a random plan from hints you give it.
  • You plan leftovers and frozen meals: Plan to Eat says it does both.
  • You plan breakfast and lunch too: ReciMe sorts the plan by breakfast, lunch, and dinner, and Paprika plans by day, week, or month.
  • You want to use it for free: The meal plan is free in Matlyst, ReciMe, and Samsung Food. In AnyList it takes AnyList Complete, and Plan to Eat has a 14-day free trial.

How we chose

We included apps that are in the US App Store and have a meal plan as one of their main features. Most are on Google Play too. We used our own judgment to pick them, and an app that isn’t here isn’t necessarily worse.

Every fact comes from the app’s own listing in the US App Store, from Google Play in the US, and from the app’s own website. We checked them on November 21, 2026. Each card links to its sources.

  • Alphabetical order. The apps are listed by name, and the order isn’t a ranking.
  • “Not stated” means we didn’t find it in the sources. It doesn’t mean the app can’t do it.
  • No stars or ratings. They change from week to week, and they say little about what suits you.
  • No prices in dollars. Prices change and can differ between the App Store and Google Play. You’ll see the price in the store before you buy.
  • No logos or screenshots from other apps. The names belong to their owners, and Matlyst isn’t affiliated with any of them.

Questions and answers

What is the best meal planning app?

It depends on whether you want to plan yourself. With your own recipes, look at Matlyst, Paprika, Plan to Eat, or Recipe Keeper. If you want the app to make the plan, look at Samsung Food+ or Recipe Keeper. If you plan leftovers, look at Plan to Eat.

Is there a free meal planner app?

Yes. The meal plan is free in Matlyst, ReciMe, and Samsung Food. Samsung Food says weekly plans made with AI need Samsung Food+.

Does Matlyst make a meal plan for me?

No. You pick the recipes and put them on the days. Matlyst then sends the ingredients to one grocery list.

Can my family share the meal plan?

Paprika (Premium), AnyList (Complete), Plan to Eat, Recipe Keeper, and Matlyst (Pro) say you can share. Samsung Food says you can share grocery lists.

Is Mealime still available?

Mealime’s closing page says it was discontinued as of October 21, 2026, and points to Meals Hub in Albertsons Companies grocery apps.
