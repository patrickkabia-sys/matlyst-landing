Grocery list

A shopping list that comes straight from your recipes

Add a recipe and the ingredients are on your list. Throw in coffee and paper towels yourself, and take one list to the store.

How to make a grocery list from recipes

  1. Open a recipe or your meal plan. Pick one recipe, or a day or week you have planned.
  2. Add it to the grocery list. The ingredients go on the list, with the amounts the recipe calls for.
  3. Shop by section. Items are grouped by store section, like produce, dairy, and spices. Check them off as you go.

It works with any recipe in Matlyst, whether you saved it from a website, TikTok, or Instagram, or typed it in yourself. You can also add things that aren’t in a recipe, like coffee and paper towels.

What happens when two recipes need the same thing

Say two recipes both need an onion. You get one line for onions, not two. Matlyst puts items with the same name on one line. When the units differ, like 1 cup in one recipe and 200 g in another, the amounts are shown together on that line, such as “1 cup · 200 g,” because it does not guess how to convert them. To tidy it up, tap the amount and enter a single amount and unit.

Skip what you already have

Items already in your pantry are unselected when you add a recipe to the list, and marked “In your pantry.” That’s a reminder, not a rule: you see what you already have before you buy a second jar. Check the pantry before you shop, and update it when you cook.

Share the list with your household

A Pro member can create a household for up to 6 people, and joining is free. The grocery list, the meal plan, and the pantry are shared and update live for everyone. One person plans, another shops, and the person at the store checks things off for everyone.

Build the list from a meal plan

The most reliable grocery list is a by-product of a plan. If you write the list first, you buy what you remember. If you plan first, you buy what you will cook.

  1. Check what you already have. Open your pantry and look at what expires first. Plan those into the first dinners of the week.
  2. Fill the menu. Put recipes on the meal plan. You do not need to plan every meal. Four or five dinners and a couple of lunches is a realistic week.
  3. Send the week to the list. On the meal plan, choose Add the whole week’s recipes to your grocery list. Before anything is added, you see the ingredients, and the ones already in your pantry start deselected and marked In your pantry. Tick any you still want. Matching items are added together when the unit is the same.
  4. Read the list once, before you leave. Remove what you have, and add the things that are not in a recipe, like coffee, bin bags, and breakfast.

Matlyst does not fill the menu for you. You choose the recipes, and the list follows from them.

How to walk the store by section

The list is sorted by store section, such as produce, dairy, and spices, so you can move through the store in one pass. A few habits make that pass faster.

  • Walk the store in your own order. Most people have a route. Do the sections in the order you walk them, and check items off as you go.
  • Do cold items last. Dairy, meat, and frozen food go in the cart at the end of the trip, so they spend less time out of the cold.
  • Do not shop the same section twice. If you remember something from a section you already passed, add it to the list, and see whether it is worth going back.
  • Use the checked state. Check items off as they go in the cart, so what is left is what you still need.

One boundary is worth knowing: sections come from a general store layout, not from your store. If your store puts something in an unusual place, you may need to go back for it.

Duplicates, units, and why two amounts on one line is fine

The most common surprise on a merged list is one item with two amounts beside it. It is not a bug, and here is the logic behind it.

What the recipes call forWhat you see
2 carrots and 3 carrotsOne line: carrots, 5
2 cups of flour and 1 cup of flourOne line: flour, 3 cups
1 cup of rice and 200 g of riceOne line: rice, 1 cup · 200 g
1 onion and 150 g of onionOne line: onion, 1 · 150 g
1 red onion and 1 onionTwo lines, because the names differ

Matlyst puts items with the same name on one line, and the store section comes from that name. It adds the amounts together only when the unit matches. When the units differ, it does not guess how to convert, because a wrong guess is worse than showing both. You see both amounts side by side and decide. At the store, that usually takes a few seconds: you pick one bag of rice that covers both.

How to keep your list tidy

The units come from the recipes. If a line shows two amounts and you want one, tap the amount. Matlyst explains that the amounts are in different units and lets you enter one amount and unit to replace them. After that, the line shows a single amount.

A week with leftovers

A list that ignores leftovers will make you buy too much. Plan them in, and the list gets shorter.

In Matlyst

The pantry shows how many days are left on each item.

Read more
  1. Cook one thing twice. A roast chicken on Sunday becomes soup or wraps on Tuesday. Plan both on the menu, and you buy one chicken.
  2. Cook a double batch of a base. Rice, beans, or a sauce can feed two meals.
  3. Keep a night open. One dinner a week with no recipe is the night you eat leftovers, and the list stays smaller.
  4. Respect the window. Cooked meat and poultry leftovers keep 3 to 4 days in the fridge, according to FoodSafety.gov, so plan leftover dinners early in that window, or freeze them.

If the leftover meals are not on the plan, they will not be eaten, and the list for next week will still include the ingredients you already have. A short check of the pantry before the next shop catches it.

Budget habits that need no prices

Matlyst does not track prices, and this page does not either. You can still shop with less waste, and most of the saving comes from habits, not from numbers.

  • Shop with a list, and stay on it. A list does not make you buy less, but it makes you buy on purpose.
  • Shop once. Every extra trip adds items that were not on the plan.
  • Check the pantry first. The cheapest food is what you already own.
  • Plan one meal around the item that goes first. That is wasted food avoided, which is money kept.
  • Look at one recipe at a time. If a recipe needs five things you will use once, ask whether another recipe uses them too.
  • Keep a few cheap staples you can cook from, like rice, pasta, beans, and canned tomatoes, so a bad day does not become takeout.

Running a shared list at home

A shared list works when everyone follows the same rules. A Pro member creates a household of up to 6 people. Joining is free. The grocery list, the meal plan, and the pantry update live for everyone.

  • One person plans, anyone can shop. The planner fills the menu, and whoever goes to the store uses the same list.
  • Add as you notice. The person who finishes the milk adds it. Do not wait for the weekly meeting.
  • Check off in the store, live. If two people shop at once, the checked items show up for both, so you do not buy the same thing twice.
  • Let the list update the pantry. Checked items move to the shared pantry on their own about 15 seconds after they are checked off, with a chance to undo. So whoever shops also updates the pantry. On the free plan, items past the first 5 wait for Pro.

When the list looks wrong

An item shows two amounts because the units differ

This is expected. Matlyst adds amounts only when the unit is the same, and shows the others side by side on the same line. Decide how much to buy. If you want a single amount, tap it and enter one amount and unit to replace both.

The same item is on two lines

Lines are combined when the names match. “Red onion” and “onion” are different names, so they stay apart. Rename one of them if they are the same thing for you.

An item is in the wrong section

Sections are worked out from the name of the item, and some names are ambiguous. If it lands somewhere odd, you can still check it off wherever it appears. Renaming the item more plainly may help, because the section follows the name.

An amount looks too high or too low

Matlyst uses AI to read recipes from posts and photos, so it can misread an amount. Check the original recipe, and edit the amount in Matlyst.

An ingredient you need is not on the list

Check whether it is in your pantry. When you add a recipe, ingredients already in your pantry start deselected, as a suggestion, not a rule. If you need more than you have, tick it in that step next time, or add it to the list by hand now.

You added the same recipe twice

Matlyst replaces that recipe’s items instead of doubling them, so the list shows one set, at the servings you chose last. If you plan to cook it twice, set the servings high enough to cover both meals.

Your partner cannot see the list

Sharing needs a household, which a Pro member creates and up to 6 people can join. Joining is free. If you are in the household and still do not see changes, check that you are online, because live updates and editing need a connection.

What the grocery list doesn’t do

  • It doesn’t order groceries. It’s a list, not a store.
  • It doesn’t convert units for you. Same units are added together, and different units appear as separate lines.
  • It can misread a recipe. Matlyst uses AI to read recipes from posts and photos, so check amounts before you rely on the list.
