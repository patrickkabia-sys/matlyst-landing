Comfort food meal plan

Seven classic American dinners, set up so Monday’s meat loaf becomes Wednesday’s sandwiches and Saturday’s roast becomes Sunday’s.

Monday buttermilk meat loaf with mashed potatoes, Tuesday pennsylvania Dutch chicken pot pie, Wednesday meat loaf sandwiches, Thursday old-fashioned macaroni and cheese, Friday oven-fried fish, Saturday pot roast with carrots and potatoes, and Sunday hot roast sandwiches. Two of the dinners use leftovers from an earlier day, and the grocery list below has everything you need for the whole week.

How to use the leftovers

Comfort food rests on staples that keep, which makes the week easy to plan. Monday’s meat loaf is baked whole, and the part you set aside becomes sandwiches on Wednesday. Saturday’s roast is cooked the same way and feeds Sunday’s sandwiches.

  • Monday to Wednesday: Bake the whole loaf and set aside half of it for Wednesday. Wednesday uses the meat loaf.
  • Saturday to Sunday: Roast the whole piece and set aside part of the meat for Sunday. Sunday uses the roast.

Rules for the leftovers

  • Cool food quickly. USDA FSIS (2020) says leftovers must be refrigerated within 2 hours of cooking, and to cool food rapidly. “To do this, divide large amounts of food into shallow containers.”
  • Keep the refrigerator at 40 °F or below. USDA FSIS (2020) gives 40 °F or below as the safe refrigerator-storage temperature.
  • Leftovers keep 3 to 4 days. USDA FSIS (2020) says leftovers can be kept in the refrigerator for 3 to 4 days. Leftovers this week are used within two days, or frozen.
  • Reheat to 165 °F. USDA FSIS (2020) says to make sure leftovers reach 165 °F, measured with a food thermometer.
  • Freeze what you will not eat soon. USDA FSIS (2020) says leftovers can be frozen for 3 to 4 months.
  • Rice and pasta are not saved. In our meal plans, no rice or pasta carries over as leftovers. They are cooked fresh for each meal.

Save the week in Matlyst

Matlyst does not make the meal plan for you, and the app does not know what leftovers you have. What you get is a meal plan made of your own recipes, and one grocery list built from them. Here is how to enter the week:

In Matlyst

Paste a link or share a post, then check the draft before you save.

Read more
  1. Save the recipes. Open each link above and share it with Matlyst, or paste the link into the app. Read more about importing recipes. There is no link for wednesday and sunday. Type the steps above in as a recipe of your own.
  2. Put them on the days. Open the meal plan and put each recipe on the day it sits on here.
  3. Adjust the servings. Where the week says “cook extra,” raise the number of servings on that recipe before you shop.
  4. Add the week to the grocery list. Matlyst combines matching items into one line and groups the list by aisle, see the grocery list. Before the items are added, you see the list and can uncheck what you get from the leftovers, such as the sauce in a dinner that uses the sauce from an earlier day.

Free or Pro: The meal plan and the grocery list are free. Importing from links is free for 5 imports per calendar month, and this week has five links. With Matlyst Pro there is no such limit, and your household can share the meal plan and the grocery list.

Questions about the meal plan

What counts as comfort food?

Familiar home cooking made from plain ingredients, like meat loaf, chicken pot pie, macaroni and cheese and pot roast. The week here sticks to dishes like that.

Can I use leftover meat loaf in other dishes?

Yes. Sliced meat loaf works in sandwiches, which is how this week uses it. Use it by Wednesday, as this plan does.

How long do leftovers keep in the refrigerator?

USDA FSIS (2020) says leftovers can be kept in the refrigerator for 3 to 4 days. Cool the food quickly and freeze what you will not eat soon. The leftovers in this plan are used within two days.

Can I save the meal plan in Matlyst?

Yes. Save the recipes, put them on the days in the meal plan, and add the week to the grocery list. Matlyst does not make the plan for you, so you enter it yourself.
