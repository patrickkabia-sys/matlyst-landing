Ingredient substitutes › Half-and-half

What can I use instead of half-and-half?

The simplest swap is evaporated milk straight from the can. Milk plus butter also works, but the sources do not agree on how much butter.

Illinois Extension, Utah State Extension, Colorado State Extension, and NDSU Extension all list 1 cup of undiluted evaporated milk for 1 cup of half-and-half.

Replacements for one cup

Evaporated milk

Ratio
1 cup of evaporated milk, undiluted (Illinois Extension, Utah State Extension, Colorado State Extension, NDSU Extension).
What changes
Four sources agree on this one.

Milk and butter

Ratio
Utah State Extension: 7/8 cup of milk plus 3 tablespoons of butter or margarine. Colorado State Extension: 7/8 cup of milk plus 1 1/2 tablespoons of butter. NDSU Extension: 7/8 cup of whole milk plus 1/2 tablespoon of butter or margarine.
What changes
The sources differ on the butter. The more butter, the richer the result.

Coffee cream and milk

Ratio
Colorado State Extension: 1/2 cup of coffee cream plus 1/2 cup of milk.
What changes
The source gives no more detail.

Oil and milk

Ratio
NDSU Extension: 3 tablespoons of oil plus milk to equal 1 cup.
What changes
This one has no dairy fat.

Why the butter amounts differ

Utah State Extension and Colorado State Extension describe half-and-half with slightly different fat ranges, and they give different butter amounts: 3 tablespoons against 1 1/2 tablespoons. We show both rather than pick one. If your recipe is picky, start with the smaller amount.

Questions and answers

Can I use milk instead of half-and-half?

Milk alone is thinner. The sources add butter: Utah State Extension lists 7/8 cup of milk plus 3 tablespoons of butter.

Does evaporated milk work?

Yes. Four extension sources list 1 cup of undiluted evaporated milk per cup.

Why do the sources give different butter amounts?

They do. Utah State Extension says 3 tablespoons, Colorado State Extension says 1 1/2 tablespoons, and NDSU Extension says 1/2 tablespoon.
