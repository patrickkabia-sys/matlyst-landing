Ingredient substitutes

What can I use instead of ...?

Missing something in the middle of a recipe? Here are the swaps we found sources for, with the ratio each source gives.

Pick the ingredient you are missing: buttermilk, eggs, baking powder, yeast, butter, heavy cream, half-and-half, and sour cream, and more. Each page says what to use, in what ratio, and what changes. If a source does not give a number, we do not give one either.

Pick the ingredient you are missing

Baking

Eggs

Baking powder with vinegar, mayonnaise, flax, or applesauce, by what the egg does.

Baking powder

Baking soda plus cream of tartar, or soda plus something sour.

Yeast

A package of active dry yeast, in teaspoons, and compressed yeast.

Vanilla extract

Half a vanilla bean is one tablespoon of extract.

Dairy

Buttermilk

Milk plus lemon juice or vinegar, cream of tartar, or thinned yogurt.

Butter

Margarine, shortening, oil, or lard, with a pinch of salt.

Heavy cream

Milk plus butter, for baking only. It will not whip.

Half-and-half

Evaporated milk, or milk plus butter. The sources give two butter amounts.

Sour cream

Plain yogurt cup for cup, or blended cottage cheese.

Milk

Evaporated milk with water, dry milk with water, or another milk.

Sugar and sweeteners

Brown sugar

White sugar, with or without a little molasses.

Powdered sugar

Granulated sugar with cornstarch, ground fine. Two sources, two amounts.

Honey

Sugar and water or liquid, and what honey replaces in turn.

Flour and thickeners

Cake flour

All-purpose flour minus 2 tablespoons, and the way back.

Self-rising flour

All-purpose flour with baking powder and salt. Three sources, three amounts.

Cornstarch

Twice as much flour. Tapioca and arrowroot also work.

Savory dishes

White wine

Apple or white grape juice in equal amount, or water with lemon juice and sugar.

Red wine

Grape or cranberry juice in equal amount.

Lemon juice

Vinegar, half the amount. Plus what one lemon gives.

Fresh and dried herbs

One tablespoon fresh is one teaspoon dried, or half a teaspoon ground.

Garlic

One clove is 1/8 teaspoon of garlic powder.

Broth

A bouillon cube or a teaspoon of broth base in a cup of water.

How these pages are made

  • Numbers with sources. Every ratio comes from a land-grant university extension service (Colorado State, Utah State, Nebraska, NDSU, Texas A&M, Illinois, Montana State) or King Arthur Baking, with a link and a year at the bottom of the page. If a source page has no date, the year shown is the year we accessed it.
  • No source, no number. Where no source gives a ratio, the page says so instead of guessing. Some pages have few numbers for that reason.
  • Where sources disagree, you see both. A few swaps have two different amounts in two sources. We show them side by side.
  • No recipes, and no allergy or food safety advice. The pages say what can be swapped, not what is safe for you.

Questions and answers

Why are some common swaps missing?

We only include swaps we found in a university extension source or King Arthur Baking. If the sources give no ratio, we do not give one.

Are these pages free?

Yes. You do not need the app to use them.

Can the app suggest a swap?

Yes, with Ask Matlyst, a Pro feature. It uses AI, so read the result.
