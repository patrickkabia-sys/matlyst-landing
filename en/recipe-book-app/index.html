Recipe organizer

A recipe organizer app that keeps every recipe in one place

Screenshots, bookmarks, saved reels, your mom's recipe card. Matlyst puts them in one digital recipe book you can search, sort into folders, and cook from.

Matlyst is a recipe organizer app. Share a recipe from a website, an Instagram post, or a TikTok, or take a photo of one, and it becomes a clean list of ingredients and steps you can search, scale, plan, and shop from. It’s free to start.

Why I built it

I never trained as a chef. I trained as a refrigeration technician.

Patrick, founder of Matlyst. Food is my love language. I just wanted to stop losing the recipes I cook it from.

As an apprentice, my job took me into some of the best restaurant kitchens in my hometown. I was never the one cooking. I was the one fixing the walk-in cooler before the first order came in. From there I got a close look at how professionals work. Nothing is random. Everything has a place, a label, and a date.

At home, my recipes lived in eight places: notes, OneNote, a recipe app, bookmarks, TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, and favorites. I had the cooking, but no system. So I built the system I had seen in those kitchens, for a normal family dinner.

The rest of this page shows what that system does, step by step, and where it has limits.

How it works in three steps

You don’t need to move your whole collection on day one. Start with the recipes you cook most.

1. Save it from wherever you found it

Share a link or a post to Matlyst from the Share Sheet, or open the import screen and paste a URL. Matlyst reads the page or the caption and turns it into a recipe: ingredients with amounts, numbered steps, and a photo when one is available. Websites, Instagram, TikTok (including short links), and YouTube descriptions work. With Matlyst Pro you can also import from photos, up to 20 per recipe.

2. Check it and make it yours

Read the recipe before you save it, and fix an amount, rename an ingredient, or add a note. Recipes in another language are translated to English. Amounts are metric by default. Switch to Units: US in Settings and Matlyst keeps the cups, ounces, and degrees Fahrenheit the source used. It does not convert metric recipes into cups for you.

3. Find it again in seconds

Search by title or ingredient, or open a folder. Type “garlic” and you see every saved recipe that uses it. Mark the ones you love as favorites, and keep the source link on each recipe, so you can go back to the creator.

What a recipe organizer app should actually do

Most people already have a recipe collection. It’s just scattered: a note here, a screenshot there, a saved video you can no longer find. A good recipe organizer does four jobs.

It gathers everything in one place

Recipes from websites, social posts, and photos are saved the same way, in folders you choose. Every recipe has the same layout, so you stop hunting through three apps to find the one you want.

It makes saved recipes findable

A collection you can’t search is just a bigger pile. Search should work on ingredients, because that’s how you decide what to cook on a Tuesday night.

It turns recipes into a plan

Add recipes to a weekly menu, and the ingredients go to one grocery list, combined and sorted by store section. Read more about the meal plan and the grocery list from recipes.

It helps while you cook

Cook mode shows the recipe as one checklist you can tick off as you go. When a step mentions a time, Matlyst suggests a timer, it keeps running in the background, and the screen stays awake. Cook mode needs an internet connection.

Scale a recipe for more or fewer people

Change the number of servings from 1 to 20 and every amount scales with it. Scaling is linear, so check eggs, salt, and leavening yourself when you bake, because they don’t always scale in a straight line.

Four situations, and what to do

Scenarios marked “Say you …” are examples we made up to show how it works. They are not customer stories.

Say you saw a TikTok where the amounts are only spoken

Situation
A creator says “a good splash of soy and about two tablespoons of honey,” and the caption is just emojis.
What goes wrong
You screenshot the video, never rewatch it, and lose the recipe.
What to do
Share the video to your recipe app and read the result before you trust it.
The numbers
Matlyst reads the caption. It does not transcribe the video, so spoken amounts won’t be there.
In Matlyst
Share the video, check the draft, type in the amounts you heard, and save.

Say you inherited a handwritten family card

Situation
“Butter the size of an egg,” a stained corner, slanted handwriting.
What goes wrong
The card lives in a drawer and nobody cooks from it.
What to do
Photograph it and import it as a recipe you can search and scale. Photo import is a Matlyst Pro feature.
The numbers
Handwriting is the hard case. Check every amount against the card before you save.
In Matlyst
Import from photo, then correct anything that was misread.

Say one recipe is saved in five places

Situation
A note, a bookmark, a screenshot, a pin, and a video.
What goes wrong
You can’t tell which version is the good one.
What to do
Keep one copy in one place, and add a note about what you changed.
In Matlyst
One recipe with the source link on it, and your own edits on top.

Say you’re planning dinners for a family of four

Situation
Five dinners, one budget, one trip to the store.
What goes wrong
Two recipes both need one onion, and you buy three.
What to do
Plan the week first, then shop from one merged list.
In Matlyst
Add the recipes to the weekly menu and open the grocery list. Matching items with the same unit are added together, and items already in your pantry start unchecked.

Swap an ingredient or make your own version

Out of buttermilk, or missing the fresh herb? Open the recipe and ask Ask Matlyst for a swap. “Use what I have” suggests replacements from the items in your pantry. You can also ask for a vegetarian, vegan, quicker, or smaller version, or type your own request, like “make it more exciting.”

The changes appear as a list you can undo. When you save, choose Save a copy, which keeps the original and links back to its source marked “adapted,” or Replace, which overwrites your own recipe. Ask Matlyst is a Pro feature, it uses AI, and it has a monthly allowance. Check the result before you cook from it.

Your pantry, and the recipes you forgot

Matlyst can keep track of what you have at home. Search your saved recipes by an ingredient from the fridge and see which ones use it. On the free plan, the pantry shows 5 items. Read more about the pantry app.

One evening I had a jar of miso in the fridge, searched “miso,” and found a miso chicken recipe I had saved and forgotten. Dinner was decided in a couple of seconds. For my ADHD brain, that matters. Dinner is settled, and I can get on with the rest of the day.

What Matlyst doesn’t do

A good tool says where it stops. Here is what to expect, so nothing surprises you in the kitchen.

  • It reads captions, not audio. Matlyst does not transcribe videos. If the amounts are only spoken, type them in after importing.
  • It doesn’t read comments. If a creator put the recipe in a comment or behind a link in the bio, copy it in by hand or share the link to the recipe page.
  • One recipe per post. If a reel shows three recipes, you will need to split them yourself.
  • It does not convert units on import. Metric is the default. Units: US keeps what the source used.
  • AI can make mistakes. Matlyst uses AI to read and translate recipes, so check amounts and steps before you cook, especially from photos and handwriting.
  • Cook mode needs an internet connection. Reading a saved recipe works offline for up to 30 days after the last sync.

Common mistakes when organizing recipes

These are the ones that cost people the most time.

  • Saving without reading. Amounts are the part that goes wrong. Read the draft once, every time.
  • Keeping five copies. Pick one place. Add a note about what you changed so next time you don’t guess.
  • Saving only the video. You will rewatch it every time you cook. Save the recipe instead.
  • Building the perfect folder system first. Start with a few folders like Weeknights and Holidays, and add more only when you need them.
  • Forgetting the source. Keep the link to the creator on the recipe, so you can find their other recipes later.

Your recipes stay yours

You can export all your recipes any time, for free, as a Matlyst file (a zip with folders and photos) or as HTML. Moving from Paprika? Import your Paprika export, and your categories become folders.

You can read your recipes and grocery list offline for up to 30 days after the last sync. Editing and cook mode need a connection.
