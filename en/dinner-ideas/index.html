Dinner ideas

What should I make for dinner? 22 weeknight ideas

It's 5 p.m., the fridge is half empty, and nobody has a plan. Here are dinner ideas sorted by how much time and energy you have, and a way to skip the question next week.

Quick dinners in under 30 minutes

  • Pesto pasta with chicken. Brown the chicken while the pasta cooks, then toss it all with pesto and cherry tomatoes.
  • Sheet pan chicken fajitas. Chicken, peppers, and onions on one pan, wrapped in warm tortillas.
  • Breakfast for dinner. Scrambled eggs, toast, and whatever veggies need using up.
  • Veggie stir-fry with noodles. Noodles, vegetables, and soy sauce in one pan.
  • Grilled cheese and tomato soup. The 15-minute classic.
  • Quesadillas. Tortillas, cheese, and black beans in a skillet.

Fish and seafood

  • Baked salmon with roasted vegetables. Everything on one sheet pan, 20 minutes at 400°F.
  • Fish tacos. Crispy fish, slaw, and a squeeze of lime.
  • Shrimp scampi. Shrimp, garlic, butter, and lemon over linguine.
  • Tuna melts. Pantry staples turned into a hot meal.
  • Garlic shrimp pasta. Shrimp, garlic, chili flakes, and spaghetti.

Vegetarian

  • Black bean chili. Hearty, budget-friendly, and done in 30 minutes.
  • Veggie lasagna. Make a double batch and freeze half.
  • Chickpea curry with rice. Canned chickpeas, tomatoes, and spices in one pot.
  • Roasted veggie bowls with halloumi. One sheet pan and easy cleanup.

Family favorites

  • Tacos. Taco Tuesday works any night of the week.
  • Spaghetti and meatballs. Make extra and you've got lunch tomorrow.
  • Homemade pizza. Everyone picks their own toppings.
  • Mac and cheese. Add peas or broccoli and call it balanced.
  • Chicken and rice casserole. Mild, cozy, and easy on picky eaters.

When the fridge is almost empty

  • Egg fried rice. Yesterday's rice, a couple of eggs, and leftover vegetables.
  • Pancakes. Flour, milk, and eggs are in most kitchens.

How to stop asking what's for dinner

The cooking is rarely what takes the time. Deciding is. Here's how to answer the question once a week instead of every night.

  1. Save what you like. See a dinner you want to try on TikTok, Instagram, or a website? Share it with Matlyst so it lands in your recipe book instead of getting lost in your feed.
  2. Get ideas from your own recipes. The Home screen suggests dinners from the recipes you've saved and what you have in your pantry.
  3. Plan the week. Add dinners to the days you want to plan in your meal plan, then add the ingredients to your grocery list.

Keep a list of go-to dinners

Most families rotate between ten and fifteen dinners. Write them down once and you'll always have an answer. In Matlyst, you can keep them in their own folder, like Go-to Dinners.

Plan four nights, not seven

A meal plan doesn't have to be full. Plan four dinners and leave the rest for leftovers, takeout, or something spontaneous. The plan holds up, and your grocery list gets shorter.

What should I make for dinner tonight?
