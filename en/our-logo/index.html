Our logo

Why our logo is ml., and why the dot keeps changing

A hand-written ml. for milliliter, and an orange dot that turns into whatever the recipe needs.

Our logo is ml., written by hand like an item on a grocery list. ml stands for milliliter, the unit that shows up in almost every recipe. The orange dot is a small mascot that can turn into whatever the recipe needs: a measuring scale, a thermometer, an egg, a Christmas ornament, a snowflake.

  2. What ml. means

Why we changed the logo

Our first logo was a terracotta M. When my brother-in-law saw it, he said it looked like a Teams logo. I wanted a logo that belonged to food, and an M could belong to anything.

We switched to the new logo in October 2026. It is the one you see in the app icon, in the header of this site, and at the end of every carousel we publish.

What ml. means

Almost every recipe measures something in milliliters: milk, stock, oil, vanilla. So ml. is a small piece of kitchen language that anyone who cooks already reads without thinking. We wrote it by hand, the way you would jot something on a shopping list, so it looks like it belongs on a kitchen counter and not on a corporate slide.

The name Matlyst is Norwegian for appetite. The logo is the other half of the idea: the measuring, planning side of cooking, next to the wanting.

The dot that turns into anything

The orange dot is the part that moves. In our carousels, the last slide shows ml. and the dot changes into something that fits the topic. Here are some of them.

Each clip is the same character in a different costume. It is the dot at the end of ml. that stays orange while everything else is ink.

The variants

The logo comes in four colorways: ink on beige, cream on ink, ink on orange with a white dot, and ink on sage. The orange dot stays the one bright spot, except on orange where it turns white.
