Holiday cooking

Holiday cooking, planned from the day you serve

Timelines, quantities, grocery lists, and safe temperatures for every holiday meal.

Holiday cooking is mostly planning: what to buy, when to start, how much to make, and what to do with the leftovers. Pick your holiday below for a timeline, quantities, a grocery list structure, food safety numbers from FoodSafety.gov, and how to do each step in Matlyst.

What every holiday meal needs

The details change. The jobs do not.

  1. A plan with dates. Work backward from the day you serve. Put every task on a day.
  2. Quantities that fit your guests. Scale each recipe once, then shop from one list.
  3. One grocery list, in two trips. Non-perishables early, fresh food late.
  4. Make-ahead tasks. Anything that can be done two days before should be.
  5. Safe temperatures. Cook to the right temperature, keep hot food hot and cold food cold, and chill leftovers fast.
  6. A plan for leftovers. Decide on day one what happens on day two.

The food safety numbers worth remembering

These come from FoodSafety.gov, the U.S. government’s food safety site. Always check food with a food thermometer.

WhatNumber
Turkey and other poultry, stuffing, and leftovers165°F (74°C)
Beef, pork, and lamb roasts and chops145°F (63°C), then rest 3 minutes
Ground meat and sausage160°F (71°C)
Temperature danger zone40°F (4°C) to 140°F (60°C)
Perishable food left out2 hours at most, 1 hour above 90°F (32°C)
Refrigerator and freezer40°F (4°C) or below, and 0°F (-18°C) or below
Leftover cooked meat and poultry3 to 4 days in the refrigerator

Source: FoodSafety.gov, safe minimum internal temperatures and 4 steps to food safety.

Holiday cooking questions

How early should I start planning a holiday meal?

Three to four weeks before for a big holiday like Thanksgiving or Christmas, and one to two weeks for a smaller gathering. The guides give a timeline for each.

How do I scale recipes for a crowd?

Change the servings in Matlyst, from 1 to 20. Scaling is linear, so check eggs, leavening, and spices yourself when you bake.

What temperature should turkey and leftovers reach?

FoodSafety.gov says 165°F (74°C) for all poultry, stuffing, and leftovers, checked with a food thermometer.

How long can food sit out at a party?

No more than 2 hours, or 1 hour if it is above 90°F (32°C), according to FoodSafety.gov.

How long do holiday leftovers keep?

Leftover cooked meat and poultry keep 3 to 4 days in a refrigerator at 40°F (4°C) or below, according to FoodSafety.gov.
