TikTok recipe import

Turn a TikTok into a recipe you can actually cook

Found a TikTok recipe you want to make? Share the public video with Matlyst. The app reads the available recipe text and turns it into organised ingredients and steps, ready to save.

Open the TikTok, tap Share, choose Matlyst, and review the draft recipe built from the video’s caption. Save it, and you can search it, scale it, and shop from it. This works when the recipe is written in the caption. Short links like vm.tiktok.com work too.

Why TikTok’s own Favorites are not enough

Favorites keep the video, not the recipe. You rewatch 40 seconds every time you cook, you can’t search by ingredient, and if the creator deletes the video, your recipe is gone.

How to save a recipe from TikTok, step by step

  1. Open the TikTok that has the recipe in its caption.
  2. Tap Share, then choose Matlyst. If it isn’t visible, open the app list and pick it there. You can also copy the link and paste it under Paste a link in Matlyst.
  3. Wait a moment while Matlyst reads the caption.
  4. Read the draft. Compare the amounts and steps with the video.
  5. Fix anything that looks off, pick a folder, and save.

Short links such as vm.tiktok.com, vt.tiktok.com, and tiktok.com/t/ are supported.

When the caption has no recipe

Matlyst reads the caption, not the audio. It does not transcribe speech or text shown only inside the video. If a creator says “two tablespoons of honey” out loud and writes nothing, there is nothing to import. Create the recipe by hand with the amounts you hear, or look for a written version on the creator’s profile or website. A TikTok without any text can’t be imported.

What to check in the draft before you save

Take a minute with the draft. It is the step that decides whether the recipe works when you cook it.

  • Amounts and units. A missing number usually means the creator didn’t write one. Metric is the default, and Units: US keeps cups and ounces as the source wrote them.
  • Steps in order. TikTok captions are short and sometimes jumbled. Move or delete steps until they match the video.
  • Temperatures and times. Compare them with what the creator says or shows.
  • Translated names. If the caption was in another language, the recipe is translated to English. Check ingredient names you don’t recognize.

Matlyst uses AI to read and translate recipes, and AI can make mistakes. Read before you cook.

After you save

The recipe is now a normal Matlyst recipe. Scale it from 1 to 20 servings, add it to your meal plan, and send the ingredients to your grocery list. Open it in cook mode for a checklist and timers suggested from the steps. The free plan includes 5 imports per calendar month, and Matlyst Pro adds more.

Respect for the creator

Every recipe saved from TikTok shows the creator’s handle and a link back to the original video, or to the creator’s profile if the video link is missing. This is out of respect for the creator, and it lets you return for more of their recipes.

Why TikTok captions are different

An Instagram caption can run to a page. A TikTok caption is short, and a lot of the recipe lives somewhere else: in the voice-over, in text laid over the video, or in a pinned comment. That changes what you can expect from an import.

  • Short captions are normal. A caption like “Garlic butter shrimp, 10 minutes” holds a title and nothing else. There is nothing more to read.
  • Text on the screen is not caption text. The words that pop up while the creator cooks belong to the video. Matlyst does not read them.
  • The recipe may be in a comment. Creators often pin the full recipe there. Matlyst does not read comments.
  • Some creators write the whole recipe in the caption. Those import well, and they are the ones to look for.

A quick test before you share: open the caption and tap to expand it. If you can see ingredients with amounts there, the import has something to work with.

Check it right away: a short checklist

Because TikTok captions are brief, the draft is often short too. That makes the check quick. If you paste the link on the Add tab, you see the recipe before you tap Save recipe. If you share from TikTok, check it right after it is saved.

  1. Is every ingredient there? Watch the video once with the list beside you and tick them off.
  2. Do the amounts exist? If not, decide whether to add your own now or after cooking it once.
  3. Are the steps complete? A one-line caption may give you only two steps. Add the ones the video shows.
  4. Temperature and time. Creators often say them out loud. Type them into the step.
  5. Servings. Set the number of servings, so scaling works later.
  6. Folder and title. Rename the title to what you would search for.

The import came out wrong: what to do

The recipe has a title and almost nothing else

The caption is probably short, with the real recipe in the video or a comment. Watch the video, then add the ingredients and steps yourself. The source link stays on the recipe, so you can return to the video.

Amounts are missing

Check the caption first. If the creator wrote “butter” and no amount, the draft will too. Add a quantity from the video, or write your own after you cook it once.

The steps look jumbled

Short captions are sometimes written as one run-on line. Read the steps as if you were cooking, then reorder, split, or delete them until they make sense.

The link will not import

Check that the video is public and still exists. Short links work, but a deleted or private video cannot be read. If the video is gone, paste any text you copied under Type it in.

The words look translated oddly

If the caption was in another language, Matlyst translates the recipe into the language you use the app in. Check ingredient names you do not recognize and rename them to what you can buy.

Keep a TikTok recipe collection you will actually use

TikTok is a fast feed, and Favorites fills up quickly. A short weekly habit keeps your Matlyst collection useful.

  1. Favorite freely during the week. It is quick and costs nothing.
  2. Once a week, go through Favorites and pick the two or three you will really cook.
  3. Import only those, read each draft, and save.
  4. Use a few folders, such as Weeknight, Snacks, and Guests, and rename titles to words you would search for.

The free plan includes 5 imports per calendar month, so picking the best few also fits it. Matlyst Pro adds more. You can search a saved recipe by ingredient, which a feed of favorites cannot do.

When a creator deletes a video, a Favorite disappears with it. A recipe you imported stays in Matlyst, with the ingredients, the steps, and your own changes. Only the link back to the original may stop working.

Respect the creator, and make the recipe your own

Matlyst keeps a link to the original video on every recipe saved from TikTok, and shows the creator’s handle when it can find it. Leave it there. It lets you go back for more from the same creator, and it keeps credit where it belongs.

If you want to change a recipe, Ask Matlyst (Pro, uses AI) can suggest a version that is vegan, quicker, or smaller. Read the suggestion, because AI can make mistakes, especially with baking. Choose Save a copy, and the original stays untouched while the copy keeps the source, marked “adapted.”

Common misunderstandings

  • “It listens to the video.” It does not. It reads the caption, not speech or on-screen text.
  • “It reads the comments.” It does not.
  • “It saves the video.” It saves the recipe and a link, not the file.
  • “Short links will not work.” vm.tiktok.com, vt.tiktok.com, and tiktok.com/t/ links work.
  • “The draft is always right.” AI can make mistakes. Read it before you cook.

Common mistakes

  • Not reading the draft before saving. Amounts are what goes wrong.

  • Saving the video link as the recipe. Save the recipe itself.

TikTok recipe questions

How can I extract a recipe from a TikTok video?

Share a public TikTok with Matlyst. If recipe information is available in the caption, Matlyst can organise it into ingredients and steps. It does not transcribe speech or text shown only inside the video.

How do I save a TikTok recipe?

Open the TikTok, tap Share and choose Matlyst. Check the imported ingredients and method, then save the recipe to your collection. You can also paste the TikTok link into Matlyst.

How can I convert a TikTok recipe to text?

Share or paste the public TikTok link in Matlyst. The app turns the available recipe text into a structured recipe with separate ingredients and instructions.

Can I copy a recipe from a TikTok caption?

Yes. You can share the TikTok directly with Matlyst, or copy accessible recipe text from the caption and paste it under Type it in.

Is there an app for saving recipes from TikTok?

Matlyst lets you save recipe information from public TikToks, organise the recipes in folders and use them in your meal plan and shopping list.

Is there a free app that imports recipes?

Matlyst includes a limited number of free link imports each month. Current limits, Pro features and prices are shown in the app before you buy.

What is the best way to save recipes from social media?

Save the recipe as structured ingredients and instructions, keep the source link and organise it somewhere searchable. Matlyst does this for supported public posts and links from TikTok, Instagram, YouTube and the web.

Can Matlyst import TikTok recipes in another language?

Yes. Matlyst can translate the ingredients and method into your selected app language. Review the imported recipe before cooking, especially quantities and translated ingredient names.
